Leonardo da Vinci, 1452 to 1519

A reference resource of the project Identity of Leonardo da Vinci: a gazetteer of every region where Leonardo lived or worked, organized in the order of his movements. Each entry records the documentary anchors that fix the stay, the patron saint of the place, its form of government, terrain and waterways, economic structure, and Leonardo's documented activity there. Every statement carries a source tier, so that facts fixed by contemporary documents remain distinguishable from tradition and inference.

I

Vinci

1452 . c.1464
PeriodFrom his birth on 15 April 1452 until the move to Florence, conjecturally placed in the mid 1460s C
Documentary anchorsBirth record written by his grandfather Antonio da Vinci on the final leaf of a notarial book: a grandson born to his son ser Piero on Saturday 15 April 1452 at the third hour of the night, named Lionardo; the record names the baptizing priest, Piero di Bartolomeo, and the witnesses A. The 1457 catasto declaration of Antonio da Vinci lists Leonardo, illegitimate son of ser Piero, aged five, among the household A
ChurchBaptism in the church of Santa Croce at Vinci is the accepted tradition C; the birth record itself names no church, and no patronal dedication for the village is asserted here
GovernmentRural commune under the Republic of Florence. The castle of Vinci, formerly a stronghold of the Conti Guidi, passed to Florentine control in the thirteenth century
Terrain and waterNorthwestern slopes of the Montalbano massif, terraced in olives and vines above the lower Arno valley. Anchiano, the traditional site of his infancy, lies about two kilometers north of the village
EconomyAgrarian: olive oil, wine, silkworm rearing. The paternal family were notaries, a profession that tied the household to the urban economy of Florence
CoordinatesVinci 43.7837 N, 10.925 E . Anchiano 43.8006 N, 10.9319 E

The record of the stay

The first document of Leonardo's life is his grandfather's birth entry, which fixes the day of the week and the hour: an exceptionally precise starting point for a Renaissance artist. The years with his mother Caterina, the relation of the Anchiano farmhouse to the family, and the content of his village schooling all rest on later tradition and have no support in contemporary documents. What the 1457 catasto does establish is that by the age of five Leonardo belonged to his father's household in Vinci. The rural commune and the notarial family are the initial conditions of the whole itinerary: a childhood spent between agrarian terrain and a profession built on the written instrument.

The structure of the city

The Florentine constitution barred the old nobility from office and opened power only to guild members: a merchant republic in form. Behind the machinery of sortition and short terms of office ran the factional contest of the great families, and from Cosimo onward the Medici governed as monarchs in substance while preserving the republican frame. A city without mineral resources sought its survival not in territorial conquest but in the multiplier of trade and exchange; the branch networks of the Bardi, Peruzzi, and Medici banks, stretching from London to the Black Sea, generated the surplus wealth on which workshop production and patronage rested.

Leonardo's activity

Training in Verrocchio's workshop was a comprehensive education of the bottega: casting, metallurgy, and surveying alongside painting. The 1472 enrollment marks his qualification as an independent master, yet the 1476 denunciation shows him still living under Verrocchio's roof. The commissions that documents can fix, the San Bernardo altarpiece of 1478, never begun, and the Adoration of 1481, abandoned at the underpainting, share a single trait: neither was completed. That fact is itself a source for Leonardo's position in the Florentine market of his first period.

1481.03 contract / 1481.09.28 last payment AThe San Donato a Scopeto contract stipulated delivery within twenty four to thirty months. Leonardo vanishes from the payment records after September 1481, leaving the panel in monochrome. No document fixes the date of his departure for Milan.

1482/83 . 1499

The record of the stay

The story that Lorenzo de' Medici sent Leonardo to Milan with the musician Atalante Migliorotti, bearing a silver lyre for Ludovico, comes from the Anonimo Gaddiano and has no documentary support C. What survives in Leonardo's own hand is the draft letter of self recommendation in the Codex Atlanticus, which lists military engineering first and places painting and sculpture at the end: a first rank source for how Leonardo himself read the structure of demand in the Milanese market A. From about 1490 the documents show him formally installed as a court engineer, with a workshop in the Corte Vecchia, engaged on the clay model of the Sforza horse (exhibited 1493), on festival production (the Festa del Paradiso, 1490), and on advice in canal works and fortification.

Movements in the duchy: Pavia, Vigevano

In June 1490 the fabbrica of Pavia cathedral summoned Leonardo, together with Francesco di Giorgio Martini, to advise on the building; the payment and invitation records survive A. Pavia's patron is Saint Syrus (feast 9 December). His notebooks record the Visconti library in the castle and the antique equestrian statue called the Regisole. At Vigevano, Ludovico's model estate, Leonardo studied the irrigation works and stepped water channels, with a dated entry of 1494 A.

The structure of the city

Milan held roughly twice the population of Florence, and its market for the arts had a different shape: a single overwhelming purchaser, the court, where Florence dispersed demand among guilds, confraternities, and religious houses. The horizontal spread of Leonardo's employment, from painting to ordnance, hydraulics, and festival machinery, answers that structure of demand. The wealth of a toll state astride the transalpine routes coexisted with exposure to invasion from every quarter, and the collapse of the regime in 1499 ended what would remain the longest residence of Leonardo's life, seventeen years.

1499.12 transfer of funds ABefore leaving Milan, Leonardo sent six hundred florins to his account at the hospital of Santa Maria Nuova in Florence. The movements of that account become one of the fixed frames of reference for tracking his whereabouts in the years that follow.

The record and the result

The material witness of the stay is the profile portrait of Isabella d'Este in the Louvre, black and red chalk, its outlines pricked for transfer. The pricking implies a workshop procedure of replication, consistent with Gusnasco's report that copies were being seen. For years afterward Isabella pressed Leonardo, through intermediaries, to convert the drawing into a painted portrait; he never did. The correspondence generated by that unconsummated negotiation is itself the principal source, documenting at once the working of Mantuan cultural diplomacy and the logic by which Leonardo selected and declined commissions.

c. 1500.03

The record of the stay

Leonardo arrived in the immediate aftermath of Zonchio, when Ottoman cavalry had raided as far as Friuli. What the Republic wanted from a foreign engineer was not painting but river engineering, and the Isonzo memoranda show it: a movable barrier to flood the valley at will and deny the passage. The conception is of the same grammar as the Arno diversion he would propose to Florence in 1503, and confirms that Leonardo's hydraulics formed a consistent defensive science across employers. The stay was brief; no painting commission is recorded.

The record of the stay

The structure of demand in the second Florentine period differs in kind from the first. To the religious houses and private patrons is added the republic itself, and the commissions span altarpiece (the Saint Anne for the Servites), state imagery (the Battle of Anghiari), military hydraulics (the Arno diversion), and private portraiture. Novellara's observation that mathematical experiments had drawn Leonardo away from painting is among the rare contemporary testimonies to his order of priorities in these years.

1503.10 marginal note of Agostino Vespucci AIn a printed Cicero now at Heidelberg, the chancery official Agostino Vespucci noted that Leonardo was at work on the head of Lisa del Giocondo. This marginal note is at present the single contemporary document fixing the inception of the Mona Lisa.

The Arno diversion, an attempt to strangle Pisa by moving the river, failed at the excavation stage in 1504. It is nonetheless one of the few dossiers in which the Signoria, Leonardo, and the chancellor Machiavelli appear within a single documentary chain, and together with the Borgia service of the following section it defines the ground on which any contact between the two men can be examined at the level of documents.

CoordinatesUrbino 43.7262 N, 12.6365 E . Rimini 44.059 N, 12.5684 E . Cesena 44.1391 N, 12.2431 E . Cesenatico 44.1994 N, 12.3954 E . Imola 44.3531 N, 11.7147 E

The record and the result

The Imola plan combines pacing with an odometer and bearings taken by compass to render the town as a true plan, an ichnographia seen from directly above: among the earliest measured town plans in existence. A circular frame carries the eight wind names, and marginal notes give the distances to neighboring towns; the drawing marks the passage from the intuitive bird's eye view to the coordinate plan. Its context is the confinement of Cesare at Imola in the autumn of 1502, during the revolt of his captains at Magione. In precisely those months the envoy of the Florentine republic resident at the Borgia court in Imola was Machiavelli, whose dispatches survive (legation of October to December 1502 A). This is the one interval in which the presence of Leonardo and Machiavelli at the same court can be fixed by documents on both sides; no document records a direct exchange between them D, but the overlap itself is established at tier A.

Ms L, 1502.09.06 AThe drawing of the port of Cesenatico is dated to the day and the hour: 6 September 1502 at the fifteenth hour. The canal harbor was the new state's outlet to the sea, and its survey tied the inland capital of Cesena to Adriatic shipping: military and economic infrastructure in a single reconnaissance.

The record and the result

The Piombino pages of Madrid II record Leonardo thinking through, on a specific terrain, the transition of fortification into the age of artillery: low thick walls, covered lines of fire. Interleaved with the military drawings run observations of waves breaking on the mole and of the transport and deposition of sediment; defensive architecture and fluid observation proceed as one inquiry, which is the signature of his method. The political context of the mission was the reordering of the Tyrrhenian coast after the fall of the Borgia state: for Florence, the defensibility of Piombino bore directly on the maritime supply lines of the Pisan war. The same Council of Elders with which the Appiani shared power was the body that administered the patronal feast, a small instance of the interlocking of cult and constitution in the Italian commune.

The record of the stay

The employer of the second Milanese period is the French crown. Louis XII's direct intervention against the Florentine demand for Leonardo's return (the unfinished Anghiari) shows his labor being treated as a matter of interstate diplomacy. The center of gravity of the period lies in anatomy, with the concentrated production of the Windsor anatomical sheets A and the collaboration with Marcantonio della Torre of Pavia reported by the tradition C; in hydraulics on the Adda and the Martesana canal; and in the design of the Trivulzio equestrian monument.

Vaprio d'Adda

After the French collapse of 1512 Leonardo withdrew to the villa of his pupil Francesco Melzi's family on the bank of the Adda at Vaprio, where the sequences of water studies and geological observation of the Adda gorge were made. That the refuge in a political vacuum was neither a court nor a city but the country house of a noble pupil marks the shift of his late structure of protection toward personal bonds: the same bond that would, by the will of 1519, determine the survival of the manuscripts.

The record of the stay

At the Belvedere Leonardo drew a monthly allowance but stood outside the great public commissions: Raphael held the Stanze, Michelangelo the tomb of Julius, and to Leonardo, past sixty, fell technical reconnaissance. The content of the period is the colored map of the Pontine marshes, Windsor RL 12684, of about 1514 to 1515, answering the drainage concession granted to Giuliano A; the survey of the harbor of Civitavecchia; geometry, above all the quadrature of lunes; and experiments with mirrors and burning mirrors. The halt of his dissections upon denunciation to the papal court exposes the normative constraints of the curial environment as a patronage system, and the draft letters to Giuliano are the rare surviving instance of Leonardo composing a defense of his own position.

The record of the stay

The terms of Francis I, the title of first painter, engineer, and architect of the King and a large pension, purchased not finished works but the presence of Leonardo himself at the court. The paralysis of the right hand reported by Beatis marks the physical close of his painting; the same account dwells on the mass of manuscripts and anatomical drawings, confirming that the labor of the last years had already shifted to the ordering of the papers. The documented work of the period comprises the plan for a new palace and canal system at Romorantin (1517 to 1518, manuscript studies and survey on the ground A) and the production of court festivals: the celebrations of May 1518 for the baptism of the Dauphin and the wedding of Lorenzo de' Medici, and the restaging of the Festa del Paradiso at Cloux on 19 June 1518, reported in ambassadorial dispatches A.

Burial and translation

As the will directed, the body was buried in the collegiate church of Saint Florentin within the château. The church, ruined in the revolutionary years, was demolished early in the nineteenth century (about 1806 to 1808); in 1863 an excavation conducted by Arsène Houssaye recovered remains presumed to be Leonardo's, and in 1874 they were placed in the chapel of Saint Hubert on the ramparts. The present tomb is thus a translation, and the identification of the remains has never risen above presumption D.

Will, 1519.04.23 AAll the manuscripts, drawings, and instruments passed to Francesco de' Melzi, gentleman of Milan. The route by which Leonardo's intellectual estate survives to the present day was determined by this single instrument.
XII

Synthesis: three variables of the itinerary

The variable of commission structure

Ordered by type of purchaser, the itinerary shows a steady enlargement of the commissioning body: the guild and monastic market of the first Florentine period, the single court of Sforza Milan, the republican government of the second Florentine period, the military state of the Borgia, the papal curia, and finally the crown of France. The differences of constitution and economy among these places appear directly as differences in what was asked of Leonardo. The guild city bought panels; the court bought festivals and representation; the state at war bought survey and fortification; the crown bought the belonging of the man himself.

The variable of geopolitics

Few of the major moves were freely chosen. The departures of 1499 (the fall of the Sforza), 1512 to 1513 (the fall of the French and the retreat to Vaprio, then Rome), and 1516 (the death of Giuliano de' Medici) were each preceded by the collapse of a regime or the loss of a protector. A model of attraction, in which the artist gravitates toward safety and the largest purse, captures part of the pattern; but expulsion, not attraction, is the proximate cause of the greater number of the moves. Leonardo was pressed out of falling structures at least as often as he was drawn toward rising ones.

The variable of water

Rearranged by drainage basin, the itinerary reads: the Arno (Vinci, Florence, the Pisan front), the Po system (Milan, Pavia, Mantua, the Adda at Vaprio), the lagoon and the Adriatic shore (Venice, Cesenatico), the Tyrrhenian coast (Piombino, Civitavecchia), the Tiber (Rome), the Loire (Amboise, Romorantin). In every place of the itinerary, without exception, some work of hydraulics, flood control, or harbor engineering is documentarily attested. If patron saint and constitution are the variables by which each city represented itself, water is the physical socket by which Leonardo's competence connected to each of them.

Several facts in this gazetteer are frequently given incorrectly in general literature. The following notes record the state of the evidence for each, so that the entries above can be used with confidence.

PointState of the evidence
Patron saint of Piombino Saint Anastasia, feast 8 May. Her cult in the town is attested from the fourteenth century, and in 1471 the Council of Elders decreed an annual palio for her feast (diocesan records; the canonical recognition of 1874 formalized an existing cult). Saint Cerbonius, often cited instead, is the patron of the diocese of Massa Marittima, successor of the see of Populonia, and of Populonia itself, not of the town of Piombino. The distinction matters here because the very council that shared power with the Appiani, the constitutional feature Leonardo's employers dealt with, was the body administering the patronal feast
Patron saint of Amboise The center of the cult of castle and town was Saint Florentin, whose relics Fulk Nerra translated into the château around 1010, founding the collegiate church of Notre Dame and Saint Florentin; the parish church of the lower town was Saint Denis. Saint Hubert, frequently named as the town's patron because Leonardo's present tomb stands in the chapel of that dedication, names only the royal oratory built under Charles VIII between 1491 and 1496 (and a suburban church consecrated in 1521). Leonardo's will and the burial register of 1519 concern the church of Saint Florentin (French Ministry of Culture, Mérimée database; Biblissima; L. Gaugain, Amboise, 2014)
Dates of exhumation and translation The collegiate church was demolished about 1806 to 1808 after revolutionary ruin; Houssaye's excavation took place in 1863; the translation of the presumed remains to the chapel of Saint Hubert followed in 1874. The three dates are regularly conflated into a single event of 1863. The identification of the remains rests on circumstantial evidence and remains a presumption
Arrival in Milan The year 1482 rests on the biographical tradition of the Anonimo Gaddiano; the first document placing Leonardo in Milan is the contract of 25 April 1483 for the Virgin of the Rocks. Any statement of the period should preserve the difference of tier between the two dates
The mechanical lion The automaton in the form of a lion, which walked and opened its breast to disclose lilies, is known through later testimony, above all Lomazzo (1584); it is associated by different sources with the entry of Francis I into Lyon in 1515 and with a presentation at Argentan in 1517. No contemporary document assigns its construction to the Amboise years, and it should be handled at tier C or below
Leonardo and Machiavelli Their simultaneous presence at the court of Cesare Borgia at Imola in the autumn of 1502 is fixed independently on both sides: Leonardo by the patent of 18 August 1502 and the Imola plan, Machiavelli by the surviving dispatches of his legation of October to December 1502. No document records a direct exchange between the two men, at Imola or in Florence; the Arno diversion dossier of 1503 to 1504 is the other documentary chain in which both appear near the same business of state. Claims of collaboration beyond these overlaps exceed the evidence
