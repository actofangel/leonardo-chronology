Corpus Geometriae

Constructions and propositions from Hippocrates of Chios through Euclid, Archimedes and Apollonius to Alberti, Pacioli and Leonardo, each extracted as a self-contained part. Every part carries its locus in the source, a separation of factual and interpretive layers, a GeoGebra command sequence, a Python construction function, and the result of its verification in sympy.

Layers

  • factWhat the source states, in summary
  • interpretationModern formalisation, attribution, connections
  • hypothesisUnder test; never treated as fact

Verification

  • symbolicHolds identically in general symbols
  • instanceHolds exactly for a concrete construction
  • numericConfirmed to 50 digits; not a proof

Citation

  • locatedIdentified down to the proposition
  • to verifyPage or folio to be checked in the edition

Elementa

The complete Elements, all thirteen books, held in this page and in elements.json: Heiberg's Greek (Teubner 1883 to 1888) and Heath's English (Cambridge 1908) for every definition, postulate, common notion and proposition, with Heath's notes. For Book I the full source of both machine-checked formalisations is included: GeoCoq (Coq / Rocq, Beeson's formalisation of Euclid's own proofs, every one of its 254 files) and LeanEuclid (Lean 4, System E; Murphy et al., ICML 2024). No content here depends on a link: the repositories are copied into the project at pinned commits. Four corpus parts also carry a machine proof from Newclid, with a note on what each proof does and does not establish.

The dependency graph is read from Heath's bracketed references ([I. 4], [Post. 5]), which the encoding marks up. Euclid's text cites nothing, so the graph and everything computed from it, such as which propositions rest on the parallel postulate, belongs to the interpretive layer: it records Heath's reading of each proof.

    Bibliotheca

    All parts live in a single Python module, geometria.py. With sympy installed, running it executes every verification.

    pip install sympy
python geometria.py

    Call a single part with geometria.run("euclid-II-11") to get its constructed objects and its checks. Paste a GeoGebra sequence into the Input Bar line by line or all at once (3D parts in the 3D Graphics view).
